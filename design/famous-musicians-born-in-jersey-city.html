    Famous Musicians Born in Jersey City

    50 documented entries, every one cited. Listed in order of era, then name. Never ranked.

    Jersey City is the birthplace of a long line of nationally known musicians: Nancy Sinatra and Frank Sinatra Jr., the jazz guitarist Al Di Meola, Broadway's Jerry Herman and Phyllis Newman, the Rascals' drummer Dino Danelli, three founding members of Kool and the Gang, and the entertainer Flip Wilson. This page lists every one the archive has documented, with the source for each birthplace.

    The record runs from the nineteenth century forward. The classic banjoist George Washington Gregory was born in the city in 1867; the composer William W. Gilchrist, the arts administrator and singer Maude Roberts George (1888) and the opera bass and actor Basil Ruysdael belong to the same early period. The violinist Bob Lido, born in 1914, became a regular cast member of The Lawrence Welk Show; the drummer Ed Shaughnessy held the house drum chair on The Tonight Show with Johnny Carson from 1962 to 1992; the guitarist Al Caiola, the choral conductor Leonard De Paur and the composer Gil Melle of The Andromeda Strain soundtrack were all Jersey City born.

    Broadway and the stage claim Jerry Herman, the composer and lyricist born in the city in 1931, Phyllis Newman, born in 1933 and winner of the 1962 Tony Award, Maury Yeston, who attended the Jersey Academy in the city, the soprano Diana Soviero and the actress and singer Beth Fowler. Flip Wilson, born Clerow Wilson Jr. in Jersey City in 1933, grew up in the city before The Flip Wilson Show ran on NBC from 1970 to 1974.

    Pop and rock begin with the Sinatras. Nancy Sinatra was born in Jersey City on June 8, 1940, and her brother Frank Sinatra Jr. on January 10, 1944; the family moved to Hasbrouck Heights while Nancy was a toddler, and their father, Frank Sinatra, is from Hoboken, not Jersey City. Dino Danelli, born Robert Daniel in the city in 1944, was an original member and the drummer of the Young Rascals. Al Di Meola, born in Jersey City in 1954 and raised in Bergenfield, reached No. 58 on the Billboard 200 with Elegant Gypsy (1977). Brute Force recorded King of Fuh for Apple Records in 1969; Frank Infante played guitar in Blondie; Howard Wyeth is best known as a drummer for Bob Dylan; Tony Velona wrote Lollipops and Roses.

    Funk and soul run through Lincoln High School. Kool and the Gang's founding drummer George Brown, saxophonist Dennis "Dee Tee" Thomas and guitarist Claydes Charles Smith were born in the city, where the group formed in 1964. The jazz saxophonist Jimmy Lyons, born in Jersey City in 1931 and raised there until age nine, went on to a long partnership with Cecil Taylor; the guitarist Vic Juris, the bassist Norman Edge, the drummer Sonny Igoe and the singer Jane Harvey were born here too. Christina Milian, born Christine Flores in 1981, and the comedian and musician Kate Micucci are among the most recent.

    An entry appears here when its own sourced text states a Jersey City birth and the subject has a national record (a chart entry, a Wikipedia article, or a confirmed Wikidata identity). Where the subject was raised elsewhere, the record card says so. People who lived or worked in the city without being born here are documented throughout the archive and on the famous people page.

    Questions, answered§

    Which famous musicians were born in Jersey City?

    Among others: Nancy Sinatra (1940), Frank Sinatra Jr. (1944), Al Di Meola (1954), Jerry Herman (1931), Phyllis Newman (1933), Dino Danelli (1944), Flip Wilson (1933), and Kool and the Gang's George Brown, Dennis "Dee Tee" Thomas and Claydes Charles Smith. Every entry on this page carries the source for the birthplace.

    Was Frank Sinatra born in Jersey City?

    No. Frank Sinatra is from Hoboken. Two of his children, Nancy Sinatra and Frank Sinatra Jr., were born in Jersey City.

    Was Kool and the Gang formed in Jersey City?

    Yes. The group formed in Jersey City in 1964 around brothers Robert "Kool" Bell and Ronald Bell and friends from Lincoln High School. The founding drummer George Brown, saxophonist Dennis Thomas and guitarist Claydes Charles Smith were born in the city, and the stretch of Maple Street where they grew up was renamed Kool and the Gang Way in 2016.

    Is Queen Latifah or Lauryn Hill from Jersey City?

    No. Queen Latifah was born in Newark and raised in East Orange; Lauryn Hill is from South Orange. Both are often credited to Jersey City online; the archive's Sound Report Issue No. 1 documents the correction.

    How does the archive decide who counts as born in Jersey City?

    Only by a sourced statement in the entry itself: a cited biography, obituary or encyclopedia entry naming Jersey City as the birthplace. A name that appears on an unsourced online list does not qualify.
