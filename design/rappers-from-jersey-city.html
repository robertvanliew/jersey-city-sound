    Rappers from Jersey City

    59 documented entries, every one cited. Listed in order of era, then name. Never ranked.

    Jersey City has produced rappers since the park jams of about 1980: the crews that battled New York, the Flavor Unit members who put the city on the Golden Era map, the mixtape MCs of the 2000s, and the street rap of today. This page lists every rapper, MC, crew and rap group documented in The Jersey City Sound, each with its own cited sources.

    The earliest rap acts in the record are crews. Sweet Slick and Slide, founded by Lord Sun Albee Al, and the Tranquilizing Three out of the Duncan Projects were, by the account of their peers, the two Chilltown crews strong enough to hold their own against the celebrated crews of New York; the Tranquilizing Three secured one of the scene's earliest record deals. Lord Sun Albee Al is remembered as a party rocker who could take over any room from Jersey City to the Bronx. Champagne, by the scene's account one of the city's first female MCs and artist managers, was for a time a member of the Chiba Crew and managed Positively Black, the conscious rap group Prince Kyrie formed in 1988 that went from the family-run Trumpet Records to Select Records. Killer Force appear on period flyers from the Benmore Skating Rink.

    In the late 1980s Jersey City supplied much of the Flavor Unit, the North Jersey collective around DJ Mark the 45 King and Queen Latifah. Chill Rob G's debut album Ride the Rhythm (Wild Pitch Records, 1989) is widely cited as a classic of the era, and his song Let the Words Flow was sampled without permission by Snap! for The Power (1990). Apache fronted the Flavor Unit on its 1990 album; Latee recorded early singles including This Cut's Got Flavor (1988); Double J hosted block parties with Apache before joining the crew in 1988 and appears on the 1990 posse cut Flavor Unit Assassination Squad. Chill Divine's 12-inch came out in 1990 on Square Records, the label founded by Stan Krause of Stan's Square Records.

    The 1990s put Jersey City rap on the national charts. Double XX Posse, the trio of Sugar Ray, Peter Barnes and DJ BK, debuted on the city's Mel-O Records in 1990; their 1992 single Not Gonna Be Able to Do It reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot Rap Singles chart, and DJ DX later took his name from the group. Attrell Cordes, known as Prince Be of PM Dawn, co-wrote and performed Set Adrift on Memory Bliss (1991), a No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100. Mr. Low Kash 'N Da Shady Bunch released Forever Raw in 1996; TGK's Avenue Ride came out on Catamount Records in 1995; Maniac Mobb, with Craiga Ski and Double J, worked the underground. Riggamortis, a first cousin of DJ Flash, is credited by community voices with sharpening a generation of MCs' punchlines.

    In the 2000s the city's rappers came up through the mixtape circuit documented on the DJs page. Ransom and Hitchcock, as The A-Team, were affiliated with DJ Clue's Desert Storm, released the Hardhood Classics mixtape trilogy, and were managed by Orlando Wharton, later president of Priority Records. Joe Budden, raised in Jersey City from age 13, reached No. 38 on the Billboard Hot 100 with Pump It Up (2003), earned a Grammy nomination, and co-founded Slaughterhouse, whose Welcome to: Our House debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 in 2012. Akon spent his high school years in Jersey City at Snyder and Dickinson before two Hot 100 No. 1 singles. CF201 has worked the underground since 1993, and Heather B. carried the city's rap voice onto national television in 1992.

    The current generation includes Albee Al, son of Lord Sun Albee Al, whose Koba (2019) peaked at No. 6 on the Billboard Heatseekers Albums chart, and his sister J SASS, a conscious hip hop emcee and songwriter. Ruga and KS (Mr Xtortion) are both documented as 106 and Park Freestyle Friday champions. Max YB and De$igner Boyz represent Curries Woods in Greenville; Mariah Lynn took the city's rap to Love and Hip Hop: New York; the Many Men Records roster and Nero's From Da Mud Thugcast carry the scene's current record.

    An entry appears on this page when its documented roles include rapper, MC, rap group, hip-hop group or crew, or when the entry is hip-hop and its own sourced lead calls the subject a rapper. DJs have their own page. The archive documents; it does not rank.

    Questions, answered§

    Who are the best-known rappers from Jersey City?

    Several have national chart records: Joe Budden (Pump It Up, No. 38 on the Hot 100), Double XX Posse (a No. 1 on Hot Rap Singles), PM Dawn's Prince Be (a Hot 100 No. 1), Chill Rob G of the Flavor Unit, and Akon, who went to high school in the city. The archive lists them with the rest of the scene, in order of era, and does not rank them.

    Is Queen Latifah from Jersey City?

    No. She was born in Newark and raised in East Orange. Jersey City is in her story through the original Flavor Unit, which included the Jersey City rappers Apache, Chill Rob G and Latee. The Sound Report sets the record straight in Issue No. 1.

    Who were the first rap crews in Jersey City?

    By the account of the scene's pioneers, Sweet Slick and Slide and the Tranquilizing Three, both formed around 1980, were the two Chilltown crews able to hold their own against New York's crews. The Chiba Crew, a DJ and MC collective of the late 1970s and 1980s, bridged disco, breakbeats and early hip-hop.

    What is the Flavor Unit's connection to Jersey City?

    The Flavor Unit, the late-1980s collective around DJ Mark the 45 King and Queen Latifah, drew several of its rappers from Jersey City: Chill Rob G, Apache, Latee and Double J are all documented in the archive as Jersey City artists and original members.

    Is Joe Budden from Jersey City?

    He was raised in Jersey City from age 13 and came up through the city's mixtape circuit before his national career. His entry carries the sources.

    What does 201 mean in Jersey City rap?

    201 is the city's area code and a shorthand for Jersey City itself, as in CF201's name or the 201 style of doubled drums named by the city's mixtape DJs. The city's hip-hop nickname is Chilltown; its documented history is on the Chilltown page.
