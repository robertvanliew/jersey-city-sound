    Famous People from Jersey City: Music and Entertainment

    112 documented entries, every one cited. Listed in order of era, then name. Never ranked.

    This page is scoped to music and entertainment. It lists every person and group in The Jersey City Sound with a national record (a Billboard chart entry, a published encyclopedia article, or a confirmed Wikidata identity) and a documented Jersey City connection: born here, raised here, formed here, or lived and worked here. Each card links to a cited entry.

    Chart records anchor the list. Kool and the Gang formed in the city in 1964; PM Dawn took Set Adrift on Memory Bliss to No. 1 on the Hot 100 in 1991; The Manhattans, Nancy Sinatra, Marilyn McCoo and Akon all hold Billboard No. 1 singles; Double XX Posse topped the Hot Rap Singles chart in 1992; Joe Budden charted with Pump It Up in 2003. The On the Charts page sets out every chart fact in the record with its source.

    Stage, screen and the bandstand supply the rest: Jerry Herman and Phyllis Newman on Broadway, Flip Wilson on NBC, Ed Shaughnessy on The Tonight Show, Bob Lido on The Lawrence Welk Show, Ozzie Nelson as bandleader and television father, Heather B. on MTV's first Real World, Mariah Lynn on Love and Hip Hop: New York, and Christina Milian in pop and film. Jazz runs from Al Di Meola and Andrew Hill, who lived in the city from 2000 until his death, to Tia Fuller of Beyonce's touring band.

    Where the connection is residence rather than birth, the record card says so (a Lived or Raised row), and the entry's title reads "with Jersey City ties". The people born in the city are gathered on the born in Jersey City page. Names often credited to Jersey City that do not belong here, such as Queen Latifah and Lauryn Hill, are corrected in The Sound Report.

    Questions, answered§

    Who are the most famous people from Jersey City?

    In music and entertainment, the names with the widest national records include Kool and the Gang, Nancy Sinatra, Flip Wilson, Jerry Herman, Al Di Meola, PM Dawn, Joe Budden, Akon and Christina Milian. This page lists every documented one in order of era, not rank, and only covers music and entertainment.

    Does this page cover athletes, politicians or actors?

    No. The Jersey City Sound documents music culture. Actors appear only where they sing or perform music, and the page says so in each entry.

    Which famous musicians lived in Jersey City without being born there?

    Akon went to Snyder and Dickinson high schools in the city; Joe Budden was raised here from age 13; the jazz pianist Andrew Hill lived in Jersey City from 2000 until his death in 2007; Richie Havens died in the city in 2013. Each entry states the connection and its source.
